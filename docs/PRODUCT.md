# Opportunity AI — Product Constitution

## 1. Problem

People seeking scholarships, fellowships, grants, employment, sponsorship, and other forms of support are usually required to search for opportunities themselves.

This creates a structural information problem.

Applicants must first understand which parts of their identity, experience, responsibilities, achievements, skills, and life trajectory are relevant. They must then convert those characteristics into search terms, discover opportunities, interpret eligibility requirements, and determine whether applying is worthwhile.

Many applicants do not know what makes them supportable.

Relevant information may already exist across resumes, personal statements, transcripts, employment histories, volunteer work, family responsibilities, projects, and other materials, but remain unstructured and undiscovered.

The problem is therefore not simply:

> How can we make opportunity search better?

The deeper problem is:

> Why should the applicant have to know what to search for in the first place?

---

## 2. Core Idea

Opportunity AI begins with the person, not the opportunity.

The system uses AI to extract opportunity-relevant information from an applicant's existing materials and convert it into an evidence-backed structured representation of the person.

That representation can then be matched against scholarships, fellowships, grants, employers, donors, sponsors, institutional programs, and other sources of support.

The system should discover relevant dimensions of the applicant even when the applicant did not explicitly identify those dimensions as search terms.

---

## 3. Core Flow

Applicant Materials

→ AI Extraction

→ Evidence-Backed Applicant Graph

→ Opportunity Discovery

→ Eligibility Analysis

→ Narrative / Mission Fit

→ Priority Analysis

→ Recommended Action

The long-term objective is not to help applicants perform more searches.

The objective is to progressively remove search from the applicant's workflow.

---

## 4. Applicant Intelligence

Applicant materials may include:

- Resume / CV
- Personal statement
- Academic history
- Transcript
- Employment history
- Volunteer and community service
- Family and caregiving responsibilities
- Entrepreneurship
- Projects and publications
- Skills
- Languages
- Geographic history
- Professional licenses
- Goals and aspirations
- Other applicant-provided materials

The system extracts opportunity-relevant claims from these materials.

Examples include:

- Transfer student
- Returning student
- Nontraditional student
- Legal professional
- Paralegal
- Entrepreneur
- Family caregiver
- Parent
- Community leader
- Long-term volunteer
- Cross-cultural experience

The applicant should not be expected to discover all relevant categories manually.

---

## 5. Evidence Principle

Every meaningful applicant claim should be traceable to evidence.

A claim should contain, where possible:

- Claim
- Evidence
- Source
- Confidence
- Verification status

Example:

Claim:
Family caregiver

Evidence:
"Caregiver for husband with cancer."

Source:
Applicant background document

Confidence:
High

Verification Status:
Applicant confirmation required

The system must distinguish between:

- Explicitly supported facts
- Reasonable but unconfirmed inferences
- Unknown information

The system should not convert missing information into assumed facts.

---

## 6. Human Confirmation Principle

AI proposes.

The applicant confirms, edits, rejects, or supplements.

The system should help the applicant discover dimensions of their own profile without allowing AI inference to silently become applicant fact.

---

## 7. Opportunity Intelligence

Opportunities should also be converted from unstructured descriptions into structured representations.

The system should identify:

- Hard eligibility requirements
- Preferred characteristics
- Geographic restrictions
- Academic requirements
- Career or field requirements
- Demographic requirements where legally and appropriately relevant
- Financial-need requirements
- Mission and donor intent
- Award value
- Deadline
- Required application materials
- Application effort
- Source and evidence for each requirement

---

## 8. Matching Is Not One Score

The system should distinguish at least three questions.

### Eligibility

Can this person apply?

### Narrative / Mission Fit

How closely does the applicant's trajectory, experience, and goals align with what the provider is trying to support?

### Priority

Given eligibility, fit, potential value, deadline, uncertainty, and application effort, how worthwhile is this opportunity for this applicant?

A recommendation should explain why it exists.

---

## 9. Applicant-Side First

The initial platform is applicant-side infrastructure.

The first objective is not to recruit scholarship providers, donors, employers, or foundations to publish opportunities on the platform.

The platform should first create value independently by understanding applicants and connecting them to support that already exists.

A provider-side network may emerge later.

---

## 10. Initial Vertical

Scholarships are the first vertical.

They are not the final product boundary.

The same architecture may eventually support:

- Fellowships
- Grants
- Educational funding
- Internships
- Employment
- Sponsorship
- Institutional aid
- Professional development programs
- Public benefits
- Other forms of opportunity and support

---

## 11. MVP Question

Version 0.1 must answer one question:

> Can AI extract opportunity-relevant characteristics from messy applicant materials that the applicant did not fully identify themselves?

The first version does not need a large scholarship database, sophisticated marketplace, or polished user interface.

Applicant intelligence comes first.

---

## 12. Product Principle

Do not begin by asking:

> What opportunities are you searching for?

Begin by asking:

> Who are you, what evidence exists about your trajectory, and what forms of support might already fit you?

The system should move from:

Search → Browse → Guess → Apply

toward:

Understand → Match → Explain → Act
