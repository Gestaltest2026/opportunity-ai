# User #1 — Application Translation v0

## Purpose
Translate the strongest observed Opportunity-specific representations into application strategy without pretending that any currently conditional case is ready for submission.

## Opportunity 001 — FGCU Foundation Scholarships

### Representation to application strategy
Primary frame:
- persistence across work, caregiving, relocation, and return to higher education
- professional-to-academic legal transition
- long-term community contribution

### Evidence packet
- current FGCU Legal Studies enrollment
- legal career progression: Legal Assistant → Paralegal → Office Manager
- long-term hula-school leadership and volunteer service
- current caregiving responsibility for spouse with cancer
- law-school aspiration and public-contribution career direction

### Narrative use
Use caregiving and educational return to explain persistence and responsibility, not to imply financial need.
Use legal-career progression to show that current education is part of a coherent trajectory rather than an isolated credential.
Use long-term community service as evidence of durable contribution rather than a one-time activity.

### Missing evidence / blocker
- next open Foundation cycle and specific donor awards
- current cumulative GPA where required
- FAFSA / financial-need status only where a specific award explicitly requires it

### Submission plan
Do not draft a generic Foundation essay yet. On reopening, first identify the specific donor-funded awards whose published criteria match the confirmed Applicant facts, then produce award-specific representations from the same canonical profile.

### Narrative use
Lead with leadership and civic engagement because those are directly aligned with the published ranking and narrative signals.
Use professional progression as evidence of responsibility and demonstrated talent.
Use extraordinary life circumstances only if relevant to a prompt; do not let them substitute for merit or hard eligibility.

### Missing evidence / blocker

- transfer GPA ≥ 3.4
- relevant newly admitted transfer timing window

### Submission plan
Before any essay work, verify the residency classification and transfer GPA. If both hard gates are satisfied, this becomes the highest-priority execution case among the three observed opportunities because the Applicant evidence aligns strongly with the published ranking signals.

## Opportunity 004 — Perez Family Study Abroad Scholarship

### Representation to application strategy
Primary frame:
- international adaptation and mature cross-context learning
- study abroad as an extension of legal/public-service trajectory
- community service as part of personal development

### Evidence packet
- Japan → Florida relocation and ESOL study
- English Literature study at Nihon University
- current FGCU Legal Studies enrollment
- law-school aspiration
- long-term community service

### Narrative use
Frame prior international transition as demonstrated adaptation, not identity.
Connect a proposed study-abroad program to a concrete legal/public-service academic and career objective.
Use community service to support maturity and personal-development claims.

### Missing evidence / blocker
- admission/application to an FGCU-approved study-abroad program
- cumulative GPA ≥ 2.8
- study-abroad credits applying toward degree completion
- at least one full FGCU semester remaining afterward
- documented financial need

### Submission plan
Do not draft until an actual approved study-abroad program exists. Once it does, the essay should be built around the specific program’s relationship to Legal Studies, future law-school plans, and the Applicant’s existing cross-context learning history.

## Observed translation rule
Opportunity-specific representation changes application strategy by changing:
1. which confirmed claims become primary evidence,
2. which claims are deliberately deprioritized,
3. which missing facts block execution,
4. which essay logic should be used if the hard gates are satisfied.

It does not override eligibility and does not justify drafting a fictional application for a closed or unresolved opportunity.
